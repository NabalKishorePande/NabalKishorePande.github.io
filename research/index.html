Research Role in the Knowledge System

P04 is the evidence and provenance layer of the seven-page knowledge architecture.

Research begins with a question or research problem and proceeds through an appropriate method, evidence collection or analysis, interpretation, and a documented output.

The purpose of this page is to make the relationship between research objects visible and traceable without duplicating the applied knowledge maintained on P02 and P03.

Canonical boundary: P04 owns the research record. P02 owns applied Scholarship Intelligence. P03 owns applied Decision Defence and Global Professional Mobility. P06 owns controlled concepts. P05 owns verified external research profiles. P07 owns research identity and authority relationships.

Research Methods

Research methods are selected according to the research question, evidence requirements, object type, and intended output.

  • Evidence collection: identifying and organising relevant primary and secondary evidence.
  • Comparative analysis: examining similarities, differences, patterns, and relationships.
  • Structured review: organising existing research through a defined review process.
  • Scoping review: mapping available evidence across a defined research area.
  • Dataset development: converting research evidence into structured and reusable data.
  • Framework development: developing structured conceptual or operational models from research findings and relationships.
  • Research execution: managing the sequence from research question through evidence collection, analysis, and output.
  • Evidence organisation: maintaining relationships between claims, evidence, outputs, and identifiers.

Canonical Ownership Boundary

P04 Owns

Research projects, research questions, papers, reports, datasets, research frameworks, methods, evidence, outputs, provenance, and research identifiers.

P04 Does Not Own

General personal identity, external profile directories, controlled concept definitions, commercial product catalogues, or authority relationships.

Anti-Duplication Rule

A research object has one canonical research home. Other pages may reference that object when required for context, but should not reproduce the complete research record.
