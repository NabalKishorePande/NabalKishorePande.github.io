Decision Defence: Make Decisions You Can Explain and Execute

Important decisions need more than confidence or information. They need clear requirements, relevant evidence, careful comparison, explicit risk assessment, and a workable plan for action.

A Practical Decision Process

Four Stages for More Defensible Decisions

Use a structured process to identify gaps, compare options, make a reasoned choice, and verify the work that follows.

01

Readiness

Define the requirements. Check your current position and identify capability gaps, missing evidence, and unresolved facts.

02

Opportunity and Fit

Screen opportunities against essential conditions, then compare eligible options using relevant criteria.

03

Risk and Decision

Examine assumptions, consequences, constraints, and risks. Record why an option is accepted, deferred, or rejected.

04

Execution and Learning

Convert the decision into tasks, owners, dates, and checks. Verify results and record lessons for the next decision.

RDE Field Manual: Practical Decision-Making Tools for Education, Career, Research, and Professional Opportunities

This manual presents practical tools from the Readiness–Decision–Execution Model. It explains how to assess readiness, identify gaps, screen opportunities, compare eligible options, assess risks, record decisions, plan execution, and learn from verified results.

Where to Apply the Method

Use Structured Decision-Making in Real Situations

The process can help organise choices across education, career planning, research, projects, and professional opportunities.

Education and Scholarships

Check eligibility, document requirements, deadlines, evidence, and fit before committing time to an application.

Career and Professional Mobility

Compare role requirements, current capabilities, proof of skills, costs, risks, and practical next steps.

Research and Project Planning

Examine whether objectives, methods, evidence, resources, and timelines support the intended result.

Execution and Review

Define actions, responsibilities, completion criteria, and verification checks so decisions translate into reviewable work.
