Decision Defence

Decision Defence is an applied approach to making important decisions explainable, evidence-based, traceable, and accountable.

The purpose is not simply to make a decision. It is to preserve the evidence, reasoning, assumptions, criteria, and responsibility surrounding that decision so that the decision can be understood, reviewed, and examined later.

Decision Evidence → Decision Rationale → Decision Traceability → Decision Accountability

Decision Defence Architecture

Decision Defence Architecture provides the structural layer for documenting and examining decisions. It connects evidence to the reasoning used, records the basis of the decision, and makes the decision process traceable.

Evidence → Rationale → Criteria → Decision → Traceability → Accountability
Evidence → Rationale → Criteria → Decision → Traceability → Accountability

Evidence

Evidence identifies the information, records, observations, or other support used in reaching a decision.

Rationale

Rationale explains how relevant evidence contributed to the decision and why a particular course of action was selected.

Criteria

Criteria identify the standards, conditions, or decision factors against which evidence is interpreted.

Traceability

Traceability connects the final decision back to its evidence, assumptions, criteria, and reasoning.

Accountability

Accountability identifies responsibility for the decision and its documented basis.

Decision Defence Research-to-Application Pathway

Decision Defence Architecture connects a research problem to evidence, a decision model, a documented decision, practical application, outcome review, and further research.

01 · Research Problem

Define the problem that requires investigation, evidence, or a structured decision.

02 · Research Question

Formulate the question that determines what evidence and analysis are required.

03 · Evidence

Collect and organise relevant evidence so that the basis of the subsequent decision can be examined.

04 · Decision Model

Establish the criteria, relationships, assumptions, and reasoning structure used to interpret evidence and support a decision.

05 · Decision Defence Architecture

Connect evidence, reasoning, assumptions, criteria, and responsibility into a traceable decision structure.

06 · Decision Record

Preserve the decision, its evidentiary basis, rationale, assumptions, and responsibility for later review.

07 · Application

Apply the documented decision structure to the relevant operational, professional, research, or mobility context.

08 · Outcome

Record what occurred after application, including relevant results, observations, and evidence.

09 · Review & Learning

Examine the outcome against the original evidence, assumptions, criteria, and decision rationale.

10 · New Research

Convert unresolved questions, new evidence, or observed limitations into the next research problem or question.

Research-to-application logic: Research Problem → Research Question → Evidence → Decision Model → Decision Defence Architecture → Decision Record → Application → Outcome → Review / Learning → New Research

Global Professional Mobility

Global Professional Mobility examines how professionals move across jurisdictions and professional systems through qualifications, evidence, capabilities, recognition, and access.

The focus is on the relationship between professional identity, qualifications, credential recognition, professional portability, and access to international professional opportunities.

Global Professional Mobility → Professional Portability → Credential Recognition → Professional Access

Professional Portability

Professional portability concerns the extent to which professional qualifications, experience, capabilities, and evidence can be carried across institutional or national contexts.

Portability can depend on documentation, qualification structures, professional standards, recognition mechanisms, and the requirements of the destination professional environment.

Related controlled concepts are maintained in P06 Concepts .

Credential Recognition

Credential recognition concerns the process through which a qualification or credential is assessed, interpreted, or accepted within another educational, professional, institutional, or jurisdictional context.

Recognition is one component of professional mobility. It connects qualifications and supporting evidence with the requirements of the destination context.

Professional Access

Professional access concerns the practical ability to enter or participate in a professional opportunity after relevant requirements, evidence, credentials, and recognition conditions have been addressed.

Mobility relationship: Evidence → Credential → Recognition → Decision → Access

Decision Defence + Global Professional Mobility

Decision Defence and Global Professional Mobility intersect where professional opportunities require decisions based on qualifications, evidence, recognition, risk, and access conditions.

A structured mobility decision can therefore require both a clear evidence base and a traceable rationale. This connects the decision system with the professional mobility system without treating the two domains as the same concept.

Evidence → Credential → Recognition → Decision → Access
